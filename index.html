ABOUT THE SESSION

Adaptation that
we can understand.

Learning does not stop when the data change. Neither should our understanding of the model.

Evolving fuzzy and neuro-fuzzy systems can learn incrementally and adapt their structure. EFS-Drift asks how we can understand changes in the data, improve adaptation, and assess whether the knowledge and decisions of a model remain interpretable over time.

We welcome theoretical advances, new methods, reproducible benchmarks and real-world studies that connect evolving fuzzy systems with nonstationary environments.

Understand change

Where does drift occur, and what does it affect?

Learn through change

How should rules, memory and forgetting evolve?

Explain change

Can predictions and adaptation decisions stay understandable?

Optimize adaptation

How do we balance accuracy, interpretability and resources?

Demonstrate usefulness

Which evaluations show a meaningful benefit?

TOPICS OF INTEREST

Different perspectives.
A shared challenge.

Contributions may focus on one or more of these themes, with a clear connection to evolving fuzzy systems under changing conditions.

  • Concept drift detection, localization, characterization and explanation
  • Evolving fuzzy and neuro-fuzzy learning in nonstationary environments
  • Incremental learning with limited, delayed or noisy supervision
  • Rule evolution, knowledge extraction and semantic consistency
  • Interpretability of predictions and model adaptations
  • Multiobjective and online optimization of evolving fuzzy systems
  • Adaptive feature selection, rule simplification and complexity control
  • Uncertainty-aware adaptation and decision-making
  • Evaluation protocols, benchmarks and reproducible studies
  • Real-world applications of interpretable adaptive learning

BEFORE YOU SUBMIT

A few useful
details.

Have a question about your paper’s fit?

Contact the lead organizer
Does a paper need to cover every theme?

No. A contribution may focus on a specific topic, provided it establishes a clear connection to evolving fuzzy systems in nonstationary environments.

Are hybrid and neuro-symbolic approaches welcome?

Yes, when their connection to evolving fuzzy systems is explicit. Evolutionary optimization is relevant when it supports learning or adaptation; it is not synonymous with evolving systems.

Are applications and benchmark studies in scope?

Yes. We welcome carefully designed applications, analytical contributions, evaluation protocols and reproducible benchmarks, alongside new algorithms.

What should an evaluation make clear?

We encourage authors to state their drift and supervision assumptions, respect temporal order and label availability, and assess appropriate baselines, computational costs and interpretability. These are scientific priorities for the session, not additional conference submission requirements.

Where can I find the latest submission information?

The official conference call for papers is the source for formats, deadlines and submission instructions. Papers undergo the standard conference peer-review process.
